Section 01

Background of the Study

Within Southeast Asia, the road network of the Philippines is highly inadequate in comparison. As of 2019, the total road length was 33,018 km with a road density of only 110.74 meters per square kilometer. This is about 8% of Thailand's road density being 1,373.77 m/sq km, approximately 13% of Vietnam's, which is 851.54 m/sq km. Singapore has the highest road density in ASEAN at 14,192.54 m/sq km and the Philippines' network is less than 1% of that. When it comes to the total road length, the Philippines has about 6% of Indonesia's 544,474 km and 4.7% of Thailand's 701,847 km. In terms of road quality, the Philippines also ranks in the bottom three among ASEAN countries, which puts emphasis on the need to improve both the adequacy and condition of its transport infrastructure (Asian Transport Observatory, n.d).

In the context of the Philippines, evidence from rural areas shows that improvements in road connectivity can transform local economies by linking previously isolated communities to major urban markets. An example is the Famy-Infanta road project; it enabled a rural fishing village to connect to larger markets such as Manila and Lucena, expanding market access, increasing demand, and generating new sources of income (Olsson, 2009).

Section 02

Theoretical Framework

The Endogenous Growth Theory (EGT), primarily addresses the shortcomings of the Cobb-Douglas mathematical function and the Solow Growth Model in order to explain the endogenous factor which is technology, however, this study will focus on road density (public infrastructure) as the catalyst of employment growth. Following the study of Aschauer (1989), this research treats infrastructure as the productive input that enhances the efficiency parameter of the Production Function. As argued by Romer (1990) and Lucas (1988) and critically assessed by Fine (2000), EGT endogenizes the road density by identifying it as a result of deliberate internal investments rather than accidental external shocks or spill over. The center of application of EGT in this study is the phenomenon known as the "Crowding-In," contrary to the traditional neoclassical views, where the public spending may displace private investment through higher interest rates.

Section 03

Conceptual Framework

This study will utilize Static Panel Data Regression as its primary method. The main approach is to employ three specific models, namely; Pooled OLS, Fixed Effects (FE) and Random Effects (RE) to further understand the impacts of the chosen explanatory variables on private sector employment level. The usage of level of employment rather than the rate is crucial in the study, as the researcher will transform the variables into natural logarithms. In this way, the coefficients can be interpreted as elasticities, showing a clearer understanding of the percentage effects between variables.

Section 04

Statement of the Problem

The study aims to determine whether a change in public infrastructure capital, specifically road density, impacts the private sector employment across 16 regions in the Philippines from 2012 to 2024. Grounded in Endogenous Growth Theory (EGT), which assumes that public capital and internal economic productivity has a positive relationship, this research examines how a change in road density impacts the labor demand of private establishments. The study seeks to answer the following:

1

Does a change in road density have a significant impact on private sector employment in different regions in the Philippines?

2

What is the elasticity of private sector employment with respect to changes in road density?

Section 05

Significance of the Study

The significance of this research lies within the real life experiences of the people. To empirically validate the socio-economic returns of public infrastructure spending in the Philippines. Anomalies about the government spending are a hot topic at the moment the researchers are writing this paper, the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) faces intense scrutiny regarding budget allocations, corruption and the efficacy of the "Build Better More (BBM)" Program. This study provides the necessary, data-driven audit of the relationship between road density and employment level.

Section 06

Scope and Limitations

This study focuses on evaluating the relationship between public infrastructure capital and the private sector employment level within the 16 regions in the Philippines. The scope of this research is specifically delimited to national road density as the primary proxy for public capital infrastructure investment. The variable includes the total kilometers (km) of primary, secondary, and tertiary roads as classified by the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), normalized by the total land area of each region. For the labor market, this research focuses exclusively on non-agricultural private sector employment. The agricultural sector is omitted from this study for the reason that employment patterns are often differentiated by the seasonal cycles and land reform policies rather than infrastructure density. "Crowding-in" effect is also isolated to private establishments now that the public sector employment is excluded, backed up by the Endogenous Growth Theory. The geographic scope of the study covers 16 out of the 18 administrative regions of the Philippines. Two regions are explicitly excluded from the panel, namely; (1) Negros Island Region (NIR) and (2) Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM). NIR is excluded due to the lack of long term data as this region was only established and subsequently dissolved during the study period. NIR has only been created in 2015; thus, there is no data for 2017-2023 (DPWH, 2024). BARMM is also excluded for the same reason, "BARMM has only been created in 2022; thus, there is no data for the prior years. (DPWH, 2024)". The temporal scope of the study spans a 12-year period from 2012 to 2024, utilizing annual data of the Labor Force Survey (LFS) and DPWH Reports.

Section 07

Definition of Terms

The following are definitions used to describe key economic concepts and technical terms within the study:

Road Density

Road density is computed as the ratio of the length of the country's total road network to the country's land area. Used to quantify the road network's extent within a specific area, usually per unit of land (e.g., kilometers of road per square kilometer). Often linked to economic development as an increase in road infrastructure fosters fostering overall socioeconomic growth. (DPWH, 2024; CBPRD, 2019)

Public Capital

An aggregate body of government owned assets used to boost productivity. Large range of asset collection such as highways, airports, roads, transit systems, and railways. Often defined as government outlay in terms of money, and a physical shock, in terms of infrastructure. (Aschauer, 1990)

Crowding-In Effect

An increase in government spending can lead to private investment. Opposite of Crowding out. Crowding-In is a Keynesian economic theory that argues higher government spending can stimulate economic activity and create a favourable macro-economic environment for private capital investment. (Tutor2u, 2023)

Reverse Causality

A condition of endogeneity where the observed correlation between variables does not flow in the hypothesized direction. The considered "outcome" is driving variation in the "exposure" and not in the hypothesized direction of causation from the exposure to the outcome (MR Dictionary, 2021).

Employment Elasticity

The numerical measure of the responsiveness of employment level to changes in economic activity, particularly output or GDP growth. It represents the percentage change in employment associated with a 1% change in an economic indicator, such as output or infrastructure density. A higher employment elasticity means a small increase in output leads to a proportionally larger increase in employment. (Fiveable, 2018).
