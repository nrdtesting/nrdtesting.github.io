01

About the Study

Public Infrastructure Investment has been considered as the driver of employment, however, the relationship between the recorded government spending and actual employment outcomes in developing countries has not enough empirical evidence to prove this certainty.

This paper examines the effect of the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) obligated infrastructure spending on the overall employment level across 16 regions in the Philippines from 2016 to 2024. The variables are anchored on the Keynesian Multiplier Theory, so the researchers expect a positive relationship between recorded investment spending, the actual completed infrastructure, and its effect on overall employment.

The effect of these investments is not direct. Government funds are committed to a certain infrastructure contract, this leads to the building of the project, which then ripples to the economic development of the country, and finally reaches the point where a worker is hired somewhere in the regional economy. Each of these steps can fail: funds may be awarded but delayed in execution, completed infrastructure can fail to translate into regional output, and regional output can grow without a proportional increase in employment.

DPWH Investment→Physical Output→Regional GDP→Employment
Why it matters now

The DPWH faces intense scrutiny regarding budget allocations, corruption, and the efficacy of the "Build Better More" program. This study provides a data-driven audit of the relationship between public infrastructure investment and employment level.

02

Research Questions

The study assesses the effect of five infrastructure investments (bridges, buildings and facilities, flood control and drainage, roads, and water provision and storage) on employment.

1

Does infrastructure investment have a significant effect on employment?

2

What are the categories that yield the most significant benefits at the regional level? And what regions benefit more from these infrastructure investments?

3

Does the effect of infrastructure investment on physical output occur within the same year, or does it manifest over a period of time?

03

Approach

The study uses a Simultaneous Equations Model (SEM) with Three Stage Least Squares (3SLS) as the estimation technique. Investment does not affect employment in one step, so each link is written as its own equation and tested on its own:

Physical Output = f(Investmentt, Investmentt−1) + Population + Disaster frequency
RGDP = f(Physical Output) + Private Consumption
Employment = f(RGDP) + Labor Force Participation Rate

Investment cannot be assumed to be randomly assigned: a region with greater capacity gets more investment and finishes its projects faster. To deal with this endogeneity, the study uses the share of a region's awarded value funded through Outside Infra channels (DepEd's BEFF, DOH's HFEP, and Unprogrammed Appropriations) as an instrument.
