Review of Related Literature

Infrastructure, Economic Growth, and Employment: An Endogenous Growth Perspective

Road infrastructure plays a critical role in connecting cities and facilitating economic activities across regions. Beyond the connectivity, it generates spillover effects through improved market accessibility, reduced transportation cost, and enhanced productivity. Supporting this is the comprehensive study of Calderón and Servén (2014) on how infrastructure contributes to economic growth. The result shows that an improvement in infrastructure, like transportation, significantly increases economic growth while decreasing income inequality. They highlighted that infrastructure reduces transaction cost, enhances market access, and improves productivity that shows the mechanisms described by the Endogenous Growth Theory. The relationship between infrastructure and economic growth is grounded by the principles of the Endogenous Growth Theory (EGT), specifically in the part of capital accumulation, technological progress, and knowledge spillovers. According to Dutta (2019) building in the framework of Paul Romer, EGT emphasizes that growth is driven internally through the investments in human capital, innovation, and knowledge. In this context, infrastructure plays a crucial role by enhancing productivity and facilitating different ideas and improvements across regions. Within the Endogenous Growth Theory, infrastructure can be treated as the form of public capital that enters in the production function, increasing the productivity in private inputs like labor and capital. This allows the infrastructure investment to generate sustained growth through increasing returns and externalities.

Review of Related Studies

According to Gibbons et al., in their study on the effects of new road infrastructure on firms in Britain, the primary mechanism through which road infrastructure affects private-sector employment is the drastic reduction in transaction and transportation costs. The study found that there was a 1% gain in accessibility due to road improvements, showing a 0.3 - 0.5% increase in establishments and employment (Gibbons et al., 2019). This suggests that accessibility due to road improvements attracts establishments that benefit most from the transport accessibility. However, knowing that Britain or the United Kingdom is a developed country, the results may differ from those of developing countries like the Philippines. According to the study of Chakrabarti in India (2018), some authors report that the impact may vary by location — Metropolitan and Non-metropolitan, Urban and Rural, locations with high and low baseline employment density. It is stated that mixed results or findings can also be due to changes in research approach. Regardless of the methods and location, whether highways can be an effective tool to promote employment remains unclear without actual research on a specific country.
